Private LLM App: Run AI Privately on Your Own Server

Every message you send to ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini is processed on someone else's server. A private LLM app changes that. Here is how to run a large language model on your own machine, with no data leaving your control.

The demand for a private LLM app has grown fast. Businesses worry about confidential data leaking into training sets. Developers want to test AI features without paying per-token. Privacy-conscious users simply do not want their conversations stored on a corporate server they have no visibility into.

The good news: running a private AI app is no longer a research project. With tools like Ollama and a self-hosted gateway like OpenClaw, you can have a fully private LLM running on your own server in under an hour.

What Is a Private LLM App?

A private LLM is a large language model that runs on infrastructure you control, rather than on a cloud provider's servers. When you interact with it, your prompts and responses never leave your machine or VPS. There are no API calls to OpenAI, no data stored in Anthropic's logs, no third-party telemetry.

A private LLM app is the interface layer on top of that model. It can be a chat UI, a Telegram bot, a command-line tool, or a full assistant platform. The defining feature is that the inference happens locally or on your own server.

This is fundamentally different from "private" tiers offered by cloud AI companies, which still process your data on their infrastructure and may still use it under certain conditions.

Why People Want a Private AI App

The most common reasons people search for a private LLM model solution:

  • Confidential business data: Legal documents, financial records, internal strategy discussions. You cannot afford this data in a vendor's training pipeline.
  • GDPR and compliance: Many European businesses need to ensure personal data does not flow to US-based servers. A self-hosted LLM solves this cleanly.
  • Cost control: Cloud LLM APIs charge per token. A local model on a decent VPS or personal machine has a fixed infrastructure cost and zero marginal API spend.
  • Censorship and restrictions: Cloud models are filtered and moderated. Local models give you unrestricted access to the model's full capabilities.
  • Offline use: Air-gapped environments, remote locations, or scenarios where reliable internet is not available.

The Best Private LLM Setup: OpenClaw and Ollama

The most practical private LLM app stack for most people is Ollama for local model serving combined with OpenClaw as the AI assistant layer. Ollama handles the heavy lifting of downloading and running models. OpenClaw provides memory, skills, scheduling, Telegram integration, and everything else that makes the experience feel like a real assistant rather than a raw model endpoint.

What You Need

  • A VPS or home server running Ubuntu 20.04+ (4GB+ RAM recommended; 8GB+ for larger models)
  • Node.js 18+ for OpenClaw
  • Ollama installed on the same or a connected machine
  • 20-60 minutes for initial setup

Step 1: Install Ollama

On your server, run the one-line installer:

curl -fsSL https://ollama.ai/install.sh | sh

Ollama starts a local API server at localhost:11434 and manages model downloads automatically. No Docker, no Python environment, no dependency hell.

Step 2: Pull a Private LLM Model

Pull your first model. For a balance of quality and resource use on a typical VPS:

# Good all-rounder for 4-8GB RAM
ollama pull llama3.1

# Smaller, faster option for low-RAM servers
ollama pull phi3

# Strong coding and reasoning on 8GB+ RAM
ollama pull qwen2.5

The model downloads to your server and is ready for inference immediately. All processing stays local.

Step 3: Connect OpenClaw to Your Local LLM

Once OpenClaw is installed, point it at your Ollama instance by setting the model string in your gateway config:

model: ollama/llama3.1

OpenClaw handles the rest. Your full assistant, complete with persistent memory, scheduled jobs, and Telegram access, now runs entirely on your own infrastructure. The model string is the only change needed to switch from cloud to local.

Best Local LLM Models for a Private AI App

Not all local LLMs are equal. Here are the best options depending on your server's resources:

Model RAM Needed Best For Ollama Tag
Llama 3.1 8B 6GB General assistant, chat llama3.1
Phi-3 Mini 3GB Low-resource servers phi3
Qwen 2.5 7B 6GB Coding, reasoning qwen2.5
Mistral 7B 6GB Fast, efficient responses mistral
Llama 3.1 70B 48GB GPT-4 quality, full privacy llama3.1:70b

For most self-hosted use cases, Llama 3.1 8B or Qwen 2.5 7B hit the right balance. They perform well on 6-8GB RAM, handle multi-turn conversations reliably, and cover the majority of assistant tasks without needing expensive hardware.

Private LLM App on iPhone or Mobile

Running the model locally does not mean being tethered to your server's terminal. Once OpenClaw is connected to your private LLM, you can access it from any device through Telegram. Your phone becomes the interface; the private model inference happens on your server.

This is the practical answer to "private LLM app for iPhone" searches. There is no iOS app that runs a full LLM privately on device for most users. The hardware requirements are too high. But a Telegram bot pointing at your self-hosted OpenClaw instance gives you mobile access with full privacy. Your data goes from your phone to your server, and nowhere else.

Private LLM Hosting: VPS vs Local Machine

You have two broad options for private LLM hosting:

Local machine (desktop or laptop): Maximum privacy, zero server cost, but requires keeping the machine on. Good for developers or power users who want local inference without any cloud involvement.

VPS (virtual private server): Always on, accessible remotely, scales with your needs. A Hetzner or DigitalOcean VPS with 8GB RAM and 4 vCPUs costs around $15-25 per month. You get the privacy benefits of self-hosting with the uptime benefits of a server. This is the recommended path for most OpenClaw users.

Private LLM vs Cloud AI: The Real Difference

Cloud AI is not inherently insecure, but it involves trust. You trust the provider's privacy policy, their data handling practices, their compliance posture, and their ability to resist subpoenas or data breaches. That trust is often reasonable, but it is still trust.

A private LLM eliminates the trust requirement. Your data does not travel to a third party. There is no privacy policy to read because there is no third party involved. The security surface is limited to your own server.

For personal use, the convenience trade-off may not matter. For business use involving customer data, legal information, or anything regulated, the math often changes.
